A system prompt is rent paid on every call

A support bot answers two thousand tickets a day. Its system prompt, the standing instructions that make it sound like the company, runs twenty-five hundred tokens. Invoices arrive per token, and the instructions ride along with every single call. Nobody reads that line of the bill on its own.

The re-reading alone is five million tokens a day. At a shelf rate of $3 per million input tokens, that is $15 a day, $5,475 a year, spent on the model re-reading the same document. The ticket bodies themselves, a hundred and fifty tokens each, come to $328.50 a year. The rent runs about sixteen times the actual work.

The first lever is caching. A cached prefix bills at roughly a tenth of the shelf rate, $0.30 per million. The same five million tokens now cost $1.50 a day, $547.50 a year. One config change and $4,900 a year stops leaking.

The second lever is a trim. Cutting the prompt from twenty-five hundred tokens to a thousand, boilerplate out, guardrails kept, drops the re-read to two million tokens a day and $2,190 a year. Both levers together: $219 a year, down from $5,475.

Rank prompts the way facilities ranks leases: re-read tokens per ticket, not how the instructions read in a doc. A prompt that costs $7.50 of rent per thousand tickets against 45 cents of ticket is a lease renegotiation waiting to happen.
